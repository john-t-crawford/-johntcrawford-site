1Load your photographs

  1. Choose your images below. They are read in this tab only. Nothing is uploaded, and no network request is made.
  2. Work through them one at a time. Each photo appears beside the drawn plates of whichever species you are considering, so you can check the characters before committing.
  3. Mark anything you cannot settle as Unsure. A guess in an identification trainer teaches the error. Unsure images are kept out of the export.
  4. Export the bundle in step 3. You get correctly named files with the location data stripped, plus the manifest and the loader script.

If your files came off an iPhone as HEIC, convert them to JPEG first with python convert-heic.py — browsers cannot read HEIC.

★Admin — pull from your phone

Photos shot in capture.html land in a private server inbox, not here automatically. Check the inbox, load whichever shots you want to work on into the bench above, and identify them exactly as you would a local file. When you confirm one, a Publish to server button appears below — that's what writes it into photos/, photos.js, and the manifest on the live server. Nothing local-only in step 1 is affected.

Commit before the next deploy sync Publishing just edited photos.js and photos-manifest.json on the live server, not in git. Running deploy.php before you commit those two files will silently overwrite them back to whatever git still has — your published photos would still be in photos/, but the site would stop listing them. Pull the current copies and commit them now.

3Export for the site

Do this Press Export bundle, then unzip it so that the photos folder lands inside /hrt/ on your server, alongside index.html. Upload photos.js to /hrt/ as well. Nothing else needs changing.
Location data is removed on export Every JPEG is rewritten with its EXIF block dropped, so the coordinates, camera serial, and timestamp do not reach your server. The originals on your disk are untouched.

Files are named <id>-<view>.jpg. Multiple photographs of the same species and view are numbered in sequence, and all of them are kept.
