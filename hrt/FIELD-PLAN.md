## The short answer

| Scope | Within ~15 min | Needs a deliberate look | Needs a curated collection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **All 121 species** | **98 (81%)** | 17 (14%) | 6 (5%) |
| **The 37 priority targets** | **28 (76%)** | 6 (16%) | 3 (8%) |

**95% of the full list is reachable without setting foot in an arboretum.** This is
not luck — the list is an ornamental horticulture curriculum, so most of it is
planted landscape material rather than wild flora, and what isn't planted is
mostly Anne Arundel County coastal-plain native.

No scope reduction is warranted. The three genuinely awkward targets are worth one
Saturday, and that trip solves a problem the rest of the project could not.

---

## Why the arboretum trip is worth more than three photographs

Every earlier step of this project hit the same wall: **verification cannot be
automated.** The manifest ships with blank `src` fields because confirming that a
photograph really is *Ostrya* and not *Carpinus* needs a human with a flora.

A labeled arboretum specimen *is* that verification. The accession tag is the
herbarium record. Shoot the tag alongside the specimen and the identification is
self-documenting — which makes the arboretum trip the highest-value hours in the
whole capture effort, regardless of how few species strictly require it.

Historic London Town and Gardens in Edgewater — about twenty minutes from you — is
an ArbNet Level II certified arboretum with a woodland garden holding magnolia,
dogwood, rhododendron and viburnum collections. Cylburn in Baltimore holds
hundreds of labeled specimen trees and is open year round. The UMD Arboretum in
College Park publishes a garden map layer. The US National Arboretum is about
forty-five minutes.

## Route 1 — The neighbourhood walk (28 of 37 targets)

Everything here is within about fifteen minutes. Most of it is within a mile.

**Old fields and roadsides** — *Pinus virginiana* (the county's default pine),
*Juniperus virginiana*, *Diospyros virginiana*, *Rhus typhina*, *Paulownia
tomentosa*, *Rosa multiflora*, *Sassafras albidum*.

**Any stormwater basin** — *Betula nigra*. Anne Arundel plants it in essentially
every retention pond in the county; you will not have to look for it.

**Commercial and subdivision plantings** — *Ilex crenata*, *Buxus sempervirens*,
*Pieris japonica*, *Lagerstroemia indica*, *Euonymus alatus*, *Zelkova serrata*,
*Quercus palustris*, *Cornus kousa*, *Callicarpa dichotoma*.

**Patuxent Research Refuge and Severn Run** — the native woodland set: *Quercus
alba*, *Q. rubra*, *Fagus grandifolia*, *Carya ovata*, *Carpinus caroliniana*,
*Ostrya virginiana*, *Nyssa sylvatica*, *Asimina triloba*, *Ilex opaca*,
*Clethra alnifolia*, *Magnolia virginiana*.

**Kinder Farm Park, Millersville** — 288 acres, mixed field and woodland edge, and
close enough to be a weekday evening.

> The *Carpinus* / *Ostrya* bark pair is the single most valuable thirty minutes in
> this list, and both grow in the same Patuxent stream bottoms. Get them in one walk.

## Route 2 — The deliberate look (6 targets)

Present in the area but you will not stumble over them.

| Target | Where to look |
|---|---|
| *Metasequoia glyptostroboides* | UMD Arboretum, National Arboretum, some corporate campuses |
| *Pinus nigra* | Older institutional plantings and cemeteries |
| *Leucothoe fontanesiana* | Shade-garden plantings; London Town |
| *Styrax japonicus* | Newer landscape plantings; London Town |
| *Halesia carolina* | London Town; National Arboretum |
| *Cotinus coggygria* | Landscape specimens; London Town — needs June to August for the plume |

Four of the six are at London Town. That is one trip.

## Route 3 — The arboretum Saturday (3 targets)

*Abies concolor*, *Fagus sylvatica*, *Acer griseum*.

All three at the US National Arboretum; *Acer griseum* and mature *Fagus sylvatica*
also at Cylburn. Shoot the accession tags.

While you are there, sweep the tier-B bark set — *Platanus*, *Lagerstroemia*,
*Carya ovata* — against labeled specimens even though you can get them at home. A
verified duplicate is worth more than an unverified original.

## Seasonal windows

Foliage characters need leaf-on, roughly **May through October**. Bark and habit
work year round. The rest are narrow:

| Window | Targets |
|---|---|
| **April** | *Cercis canadensis* in bloom on bare stems |
| **June – August** | *Cotinus coggygria* smoke plume |
| **September – November** | *Callicarpa dichotoma* violet drupes |
| **October – November** | *Hydrangea quercifolia* and *Nyssa sylvatica* autumn colour |
| **November – February** | *Ilex verticillata* fruit on bare stems |
| **December – March** | Deciduous habit silhouettes; winter twig and bud characters |

The field tool filters by all of this. **In season now** shows only what is
shootable this month; **walk out the door** hides anything needing a trip.

## A realistic estimate

The original version of this section counted outings on the assumption that
capture itself was the slow part. It isn't. The first real session — 94
photographs in 38 minutes, see the session log below — makes that plain: a
single evening at one location can produce more frames than this whole
document once budgeted for "two evening walks." Shooting is cheap. Nobody
needs six trips' worth of *time* to get a camera in front of 37 species.

What doesn't get cheaper is the thing capture speed was never going to fix:

- **Route 3 is a place, not a pace.** *Abies concolor*, *Fagus sylvatica*, and
  *Acer griseum* are arboretum-only in this list. No amount of shooting
  efficiency at home substitutes for standing in front of the accession tag —
  that trip still has to happen on its own, at Cylburn or the National
  Arboretum.
- **The seasonal-window table is a calendar, not a to-do list.** *Cercis*
  bloom, the *Cotinus* plume, *Callicarpa* drupes, *Ilex verticillata* fruit,
  and fall colour each exist for a matter of weeks. Shooting 94 photos in one
  April session does not conjure November berries — those characters are
  gated by phenology, not by how fast you can work a camera.

So the honest framing is: **one or two sessions plausibly clear most of
Routes 1 and 2** (28 + 6 of the 37) given the pace this morning demonstrated,
**one arboretum outing** clears Route 3 whenever it's convenient to schedule,
and **the seasonal returns remain seasonal** — not because a return trip is
slow, but because the subject isn't there yet. The year-long cadence in this
document was never really about six trips' worth of effort; it's four
calendar windows and one place you have to physically go.

## Before you publish

Set location to **off** or **approximate** in the field tool for anything shot at
home. Exact coordinates from your own garden are your home address, and this bundle
is meant to go on the site.

Confirm every species against the Maryland Plant Atlas or a USDA PLANTS profile
before it goes in `./photos/`. An arboretum tag counts. A gardening blog does not.
